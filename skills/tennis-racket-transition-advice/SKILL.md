# Tennis Racket Transition Advice

Use this skill when Clemente asks about tennis racquets, strings, launch angle, depth control, switching frames, or technique adjustments caused by equipment changes.

## Setup

- Prefer current authoritative sources for named racquets and strings:
  - manufacturer product pages/spec sheets,
  - reputable retailer spec tables,
  - string manufacturer pages,
  - coach/stringer references when discussing setup tradeoffs.
- Search Memoriki for Clemente’s tennis context: current racquets, strings, tension, shoes, playing style, injuries, and preferences.
- If search is unavailable, explicitly avoid exact numeric claims and frame guidance as general tennis/racquet-fitting advice.
- Do not invent specs such as weight, swingweight, beam width, stiffness, pattern, balance, or recommended tensions.

## What to ask first

Get enough context before giving precise equipment advice:

- Exact racquet models/years/versions, including head size and weight suffix.
- Current and previous string, gauge, tension, and string age.
- Whether the frame is used/old and when it was last restrung.
- Usual stroke style: flat vs topspin, eastern/semi-western/western forehand, one/two-handed backhand.
- Main symptom: balls launching high, flying long, netting, loss of control, arm pain, timing problems, serves/volleys affected.
- Court surface and competitive context if relevant.

If the user does not know, give a staged troubleshooting plan and ask for photos of the frame/string label if helpful.

## Core concepts

- **Launch angle** is influenced by racquet head geometry, string pattern, string type/tension/age, swing path, contact point, and face angle.
- **Power frames** give easier depth; players often need to spend more swing speed on spin/shape rather than forward drive.
- **Control/flexible frames** may reward driving through the ball more directly, but can demand more player-generated depth.
- **Old strings can dominate the feel**: dead poly, loose synthetic gut/multifilament, or unknown tension can make depth and launch unpredictable.
- Do not solve excessive power by decelerating; that often worsens timing and leaves the racquet face open.

## Workflow

### 1. Verify or bound the equipment comparison

When possible, verify the exact models. Then compare only supported traits:

- head size and string pattern,
- beam/stiffness/power level,
- static weight and swingweight,
- balance and maneuverability,
- typical player profile.

If exact model/year is unknown, say so:

> “I can give general Percept → E-Zone guidance, but exact launch/power differences depend on the model, year, string, and tension.”

### 2. Diagnose strings before technique

For an old or borrowed racquet:

- Ask when it was last restrung.
- Treat unknown/dead strings as a likely confounder.
- Suggest restringing before overhauling strokes.
- To tame launch/depth, consider:
  - fresh strings,
  - slightly higher tension than the previous setup,
  - thicker gauge,
  - lower-powered/control-oriented string,
  - hybrid setup if comfort is needed.

Avoid numeric tension changes unless you know the current setup and frame/string constraints; otherwise suggest consulting a stringer.

### 3. Give safe technique cues for higher launch/free power

When the new frame sends balls high or long:

- Keep swing commitment; do **not** simply slow down.
- Convert more racquet-head speed into spin and shape.
- Close the racquet face slightly, only a few degrees.
- Make contact a bit farther in front.
- Feel the strings slightly more “over” the ball.
- Finish more across/around the body when appropriate.
- Aim lower and shorter at first while recalibrating.

Use “same commitment, more shape” as the central cue.

### 4. Recalibrate targets

Have Clemente temporarily change target windows:

- Aim 2–4 feet over the net rather than using a high safety arc if balls are sailing.
- Aim for 4–6 feet inside the baseline until depth is predictable.
- Start crosscourt with margin before testing down-the-line or winner attempts.
- Judge the session by depth control and repeatability, not winners.

### 5. Prescribe calibration drills

Use drills that isolate depth and shape:

1. **Crosscourt depth drill**: 20 balls aiming 4–6 feet inside the baseline.
2. **Heavy-shape drill**: 20 balls with clear net clearance and topspin, focusing on dive.
3. **Alternation drill**: alternate one higher/heavier ball and one lower/flatter ball.
4. **Target ladder**: service line, mid-court, deep safe, then back down.
5. **String sanity test**: after restringing, repeat the same drill to separate equipment from stroke changes.

### 6. Decide: adapt, restring, or switch back

After 2–4 hitting sessions with known strings:

- If depth control improves, continue adapting.
- If balls still launch unpredictably, adjust string/tension/gauge or consider a more control-oriented frame.
- If arm discomfort appears, stop and reassess strings/tension/stiffness; do not push through equipment-related pain.
- If match results matter soon, use the familiar racquet for competition while testing the new setup in practice.

## Examples

### Moving from control frame to lively frame

“The new frame may be giving you free depth. Do not decelerate. Keep racquet-head speed, close the face slightly, contact more in front, and use spin to make the ball dive. For two sessions, aim shorter than feels natural and recalibrate your neutral rally ball.”

### Unknown old racquet strings

“Before treating this as a technique problem, restring it. Unknown old strings can make launch angle and depth feel random. Use a known setup first; then compare frames.”

### When exact specs cannot be verified

“I tried to verify exact current specs but search is unavailable, so I won’t claim numbers. Generally, this model family is more power-oriented, but exact advice depends on your version and string setup.”

## Validation checklist

Before answering:

- [ ] Did you ask for exact model/year and string/tension/age?
- [ ] Did you verify specs or label uncertainty?
- [ ] Did you check whether old strings could explain the symptom?
- [ ] Did you avoid invented racquet/string numbers?
- [ ] Did you give technique cues that preserve commitment rather than encouraging deceleration?
- [ ] Did you include calibration drills and target windows?
- [ ] Did you mention pain/arm-safety stop rules when stiffness/tension/poly are relevant?
