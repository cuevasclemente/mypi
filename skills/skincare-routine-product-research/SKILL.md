# Skincare Routine Product Research

Use this skill when Clemente asks for a skincare routine, asks how to use products such as COSRX snail mucin/cream, compares sunscreens or retinoids, or wants Korean-skincare-oriented recommendations.

## Setup

- Check Memoriki/MemPalace for prior skincare, shaving, sunscreen, acne, irritation, fragrance, and product-preference context.
- Use current authoritative sources for named products when possible:
  - official brand product pages,
  - official retailer listings,
  - ingredient/Drug Facts panels,
  - dermatologist or medical references for active ingredients.
- If ExaSearch is unavailable or out of credits, use direct product pages or clearly say that availability/specs could not be fully verified.
- Do not read or expose private account/order data. Avoid affiliate links and tracking URLs.

## Safety boundaries

- Do not diagnose skin disease or replace a dermatologist.
- Escalate to clinician/dermatologist for severe acne, painful cysts, infection signs, persistent dermatitis, rapidly changing lesions, suspected allergic reactions, or medication-level treatment questions.
- Retinoids are not recommended in pregnancy/breastfeeding unless a clinician approves; flag this when recommending retinol/retinal/adapalene/tretinoin.
- Sunscreen is non-negotiable when using retinoids, exfoliating acids, or photosensitizing products.

## Workflow

### 1. Clarify the routine goal

Ask or infer only what is necessary:
- Main concerns: dryness, shaving irritation, acne/ingrowns, redness, hyperpigmentation, texture, oiliness, anti-aging.
- Skin tolerance: sensitive/fragrance-reactive, eczema/rosacea history, shaving frequency.
- Current products and order of use.
- Preference constraints: Korean skincare, Costco/Target/Olive Young/YesStyle availability, budget, cruelty-free/fragrance-free.

For a quick answer, give a simple baseline and mark optional upgrades.

### 2. Verify named products and avoid invented specs

For products Clemente names or products you recommend:
- Fetch or inspect official pages when possible.
- Confirm product type, key actives, size/price if relevant, and usage instructions.
- For sunscreen, distinguish:
  - **US Drug Facts/FDA-filter versions** (e.g., avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate), from
  - **imported/Korean/EU-style SPF50+ PA++++ formulas** using newer filters such as DHHB, ethylhexyl triazone, or MBBT.
- If a Costco/marketplace result appears stale or region-specific, say so and suggest stable sources.

### 3. Build the minimum effective routine

Start simple and barrier-friendly:

**Morning**
1. Gentle cleanser or rinse.
2. Hydrating/barrier step if useful, e.g. snail mucin/cream, glycerin, panthenol, ceramides.
3. Moisturizer if sunscreen is not moisturizing enough.
4. Broad-spectrum sunscreen, with enough product and reapplication for outdoor exposure.

**Night**
1. Cleanse, especially if sunscreen was worn.
2. Treatment only if needed: retinoid, acne treatment, exfoliant, pigment treatment.
3. COSRX snail/barrier product.
4. Moisturizer.

After shaving: prefer soothing/hydrating/barrier products first; avoid retinoids/acids immediately after shaving.

### 4. Introduce actives slowly

Do not add multiple strong actives at once.

**Retinoid starter pattern**
- Pick one: beginner retinol, retinal, adapalene for acne/ingrowns, or prescription tretinoin only with clinician context.
- Use at night only, on dry skin.
- Start 1–2 nights/week for 2–3 weeks; increase only if calm.
- Pea-sized amount for whole face unless product instructions say otherwise.
- Avoid same night as AHA/BHA, scrubs, benzoyl peroxide unless specifically planned, or immediately after shaving.
- Use moisturizer “sandwiching” for irritation-prone skin.

**Patch/usage test**
- Patch test new products when sensitivity is plausible.
- Add one new product every 1–2 weeks so reactions can be attributed.

### 5. Recommend products by lane, not hype

Separate recommendations:
- **Barrier/hydration:** snail mucin/cream, ceramide creams, panthenol/cica products.
- **Sunscreen:** daily comfort vs sweat/outdoor vs serious UVA/long-UVA protection.
- **Retinoids:** gentle starter retinol, stronger retinal ladder, adapalene for acne-prone/ingrowns, prescription pathway.
- **Acne/ingrowns:** salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, adapalene, shaving-technique changes.

Explain why each fits the routine and what tradeoff it introduces.

### 6. Give concrete sequencing and stop rules

Include exact order and frequency, plus when to pause:
- burning that persists,
- worsening rash/flaking despite reduced frequency,
- swelling/hives,
- eye irritation from retinoid migration,
- sunscreen stinging or suspected allergy.

## Examples

### COSRX snail + shaving baseline

“Use COSRX snail after shaving as a soothing/hydrating step, then moisturizer if you feel tight. Keep retinoids and acids away from freshly shaved skin. In the morning, sunscreen is the main anti-aging product.”

### Korean sunscreen verification

“If the label has US Drug Facts with avobenzone/homosalate/octisalate, it is likely the US-compliant version, not the imported PA++++ formula. That may still be fine for daily use, but if your goal is modern filters, check the INCI list for DHHB, ethylhexyl triazone, or MBBT and buy from a reputable importer/brand store.”

### Retinoid recommendation framing

“For Korean + easy, start with a low-strength retinol from a brand you already tolerate. For performance/value, a retinal product may be more efficient, but introduce it slowly. If acne/ingrowns are the main issue, OTC adapalene has stronger acne evidence than cosmetic retinol.”
