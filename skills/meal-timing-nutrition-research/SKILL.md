# Meal Timing & Nutrition Research

Use this skill when Clemente asks about breakfast, intermittent fasting, meal timing, calorie targets, weight-loss strategy, hunger/energy during hectic days, protein/fiber/satiety, or what current evidence says about eating schedules.

## Setup

1. **Clarify the decision**
   - Examples: add breakfast vs keep usual ~1pm + ~5pm meals; maintain IF but adjust composition; improve energy on hectic days; lose weight without rebound overeating.
   - Ask about constraints if missing: wake/sleep, training timing, hunger/energy dips, weight goal, food preferences, prep capacity, and medical constraints.
2. **Use current evidence**
   - Search recent authoritative sources before research claims: ExaSearch, PubMed, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, RCTs, and official guidance where relevant.
   - Note study population and evidence type; do not overgeneralize from observational studies.
3. **Keep medical boundaries**
   - Do not diagnose or prescribe medical treatment.
   - Recommend clinician/dietitian input for diabetes/hypoglycemia, kidney/GI disease, pregnancy, eating-disorder history, appetite/weight-loss meds, insulin/sulfonylureas, dizziness/fainting, binge/restrict cycles, severe fatigue, or unexplained weight change.

## Workflow

### 1. Frame breakfast and intermittent fasting evidence

Distinguish:
- **Observational breakfast-skipping associations**: may show higher BMI or worse markers, but are confounded by sleep, stress, diet quality, socioeconomic factors, smoking, dieting attempts, and activity.
- **RCTs/systematic reviews**: more useful for causal claims about meal timing, calorie intake, weight loss, appetite, and metabolic markers.

Useful framing:
- Breakfast is not inherently necessary or fattening.
- Intermittent fasting is an adherence tool for some people, not magic.
- Total calories, protein, fiber, diet quality, and schedule fit usually dominate.

### 2. Center Clemente’s practical variables

Prioritize:
- **Total calorie intake**: breakfast helps if it reduces later overeating or improves food choices; hurts if it simply adds calories.
- **Protein**: especially during weight loss. If adding breakfast, make it protein-forward.
- **Fiber/satiety**: combine protein + fiber + some fat for durable fullness.
- **Schedule fit**: hectic days may justify an “emergency breakfast”; easy IF days may not need breakfast.
- **Energy/cognition**: ask whether fasting causes morning low energy, irritability, poor focus, or rebound eating.
- **Exercise timing**: adapt protein/carbs to performance and recovery without overclaiming.

### 3. Recommend bounded n=1 experiments

When uncertain, propose a 2–3 week experiment:
- Keep lunch/dinner broadly similar.
- Add a small protein-forward breakfast on selected days, or try delayed breakfast around 10–11am.
- Track morning energy/focus, hunger before 1pm, total calories/portions, protein/fiber, evening cravings/snacking, adherence, and weekly weight trend.

Example variants:
- 250–400 kcal breakfast with 25–40g protein when feasible.
- Greek yogurt/skyr + berries + nuts/chia; eggs/tofu + vegetables; protein smoothie; overnight oats with protein; cottage cheese + fruit; protein bar/shake + fruit for hectic mornings.
- Keep IF but improve first-meal protein/fiber.

### 4. Present tradeoffs clearly

A strong answer includes:
- Bottom-line recommendation.
- Evidence summary with confidence level.
- Application to Clemente’s current pattern.
- Practical options.
- Experiment/tracking plan.
- Medical caveats.

Avoid one-size-fits-all slogans like “breakfast is the most important meal,” “skipping breakfast causes weight gain,” “IF boosts metabolism,” or “never eat before noon.”

## Examples

### Would adding breakfast help weight loss?

1. It depends mostly on whether breakfast lowers or raises total daily intake.
2. Observational breakfast-skipping literature is confounded; RCTs are mixed and do not show a universal breakfast advantage.
3. If Clemente’s 1pm + 5pm pattern is easy and energy is fine, there is no strong reason to add breakfast.
4. If hectic days cause low energy or later overeating, test a small protein-forward breakfast.

### Is intermittent fasting supported?

Current evidence generally suggests time-restricted eating can work when it helps reduce calories, but it is not clearly superior to ordinary calorie restriction when calories/protein are matched. For Clemente, judge whether the eating window improves adherence without hurting energy, protein intake, or social/schedule fit.

## Validation Checklist

- [ ] Search current literature or authoritative guidance for research claims.
- [ ] Distinguish RCT/systematic-review evidence from observational associations.
- [ ] Avoid universal good/bad claims about breakfast or IF.
- [ ] Focus on calories, adherence, protein, fiber, satiety, and schedule fit.
- [ ] Tailor to Clemente’s usual 1pm + 5pm / IF pattern when relevant.
- [ ] Offer a practical n=1 experiment when uncertainty remains.
- [ ] Include clinician/dietitian caveats for medical risk cases.
- [ ] Avoid requesting unnecessary private medical details.
