# Personal Style Taste Development

Use a transparent experiment, not a style verdict. Help the participant notice patterns while preserving uncertainty, context, agency, and privacy.

## Start

1. Ask what the participant wants to learn and where they expect to wear the clothes.
2. Agree on photo, storage, sharing, and retention boundaries before requesting images. Prefer the minimum visual information needed.
3. Ask about practical constraints and social-safety concerns without treating caution, identity, or discomfort as pathology.
4. Choose one comparison variable. Keep other variables as similar as practical.

Read [references/METHOD.md](references/METHOD.md) before running a multi-round exercise or interpreting results.

## Run the exercise

1. **Prepare a controlled set.** Use neutral option IDs. Balance presentation order. Log unavoidable differences in lighting, pose, body, crop, fit, styling, image quality, and context.
2. **Delay the reveal.** Hide brand, price, category labels, popularity, intended conclusion, and other biasing metadata until the participant locks initial judgments.
3. **Record three layers separately.**
   - **Evidence:** directly visible or explicitly reported facts.
   - **Interpretation:** a tentative explanation of that evidence.
   - **Preference:** the participant's reaction or choice.
4. **Ask both judgments.** “Do you admire this?” and “Would you want to inhabit this in a real context?” Do not turn admiration into a recommendation.
5. **Reveal and revise.** Record what changed after metadata or confounds became known. Append corrections; do not silently rewrite earlier records.
6. **Replicate.** Retest the same proposed pattern with new examples, another day, or another realistic context. Treat a one-round result as provisional.
7. **Transfer before shopping.** Test the pattern with clothes already owned through recombination, styling, temporary adjustments, or tailoring assessment. Recommend acquisition only for a defined gap that existing items cannot test or fill.

## Output standard

Report:

- the question and controlled variable;
- evidence, interpretation, and preference in distinct fields;
- admire and inhabit results separately;
- confounds, reveal effects, corrections, and missing evidence;
- replication status and confidence;
- an existing-wardrobe experiment;
- any unresolved contextual or social-safety constraint;
- the smallest justified next step.

## Boundaries

- Do not infer personality, gender, sexuality, health, class, ethnicity, or other sensitive traits from clothes or photos.
- Do not diagnose fear, insecurity, or avoidance. A wish to reduce unwanted attention, discrimination, conflict, exposure, or physical risk can be a legitimate design constraint.
- Do not present aesthetic interpretation as objective fact or social convention as a universal rule.
- Do not identify a person, place, account, or precise location from visual clues.
- Do not reuse, publish, or transmit images beyond the agreed scope. If storage or deletion cannot be verified, say so.
- Do not let uncertain image evidence support a high-cost purchase.
